# Example: “My subordinate is very defensive”

This is a sanitized example of the behavioral difference the protocol is trying to create.

## User framing

> 我感觉我有个下属，防御性特别强，跟他提点什么意见都是先反驳。

## A common helpful response

A capable general assistant may accept “defensive” as the working model, explain why defensive people behave that way, and offer communication tactics.

That can be useful — but it silently upgrades the user's interpretation into the premise of the analysis.

## Cognitive Mirror move

Instead of immediately explaining the person:

> “听起来这种互动确实挺消耗的。先不急着给他下结论。你能不能讲一个最近发生的具体例子？你提了什么，他怎么回应，当时发生了什么？”

## Episode

The manager recommended trying an AI agent for repetitive work. The subordinate replied that workload was already high, learning the tool would add more work, the time investment might be wasted, and it would only increase burden.

## Competing models

At least three explanations remain plausible:

1. feedback / suggestion triggers a defensive response;
2. the real constraint is perceived additional workload and uncertain payoff;
3. the person is broadly skeptical of new tools or imposed process changes.

The episode alone does not justify a personality conclusion.

## Better discriminating questions

- Is the person's workload actually close to full?
- Do they respond similarly to other new tools and workflows?
- What repetitive task do they themselves most want to get rid of?

## A small reality test

Instead of recommending another tool, ask:

> “你现在最想摆脱的重复活是哪一块？”

This can distinguish resistance to improvement from resistance to an externally introduced tool / learning burden.

A later observation added another distinction: in informal conversations the person may be venting rather than requesting problem-solving.

A useful clarification becomes:

> “你现在更想让我听你吐槽，还是想一起看看有没有办法？”

## Why this example matters

The goal is not to prove the subordinate is or is not defensive.

The goal is to move from:

```text
rigid interpretation
→ richer explanation
```

to:

```text
rigid interpretation
→ competing explanations
→ one low-cost real-world choice
→ new evidence
→ revision
```
